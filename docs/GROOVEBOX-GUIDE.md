# Spelunkr Groovebox: the guide

Make vaporwave, lo-fi and chiptune tracks in your browser, for the channel. No account, nothing to install: your work
saves in this browser, and a link shares it.

## Start

1. Press **SWITCH ON**. Headphones on: the starter song is ready.
2. Press **PLAY** (or the space bar). Press it again to stop.
3. **HELP ▸ TOUR** at the top runs the tour again: it walks through a whole song, from a blank page to the export.
   Hover over anything on the page to see what it does (**HELP ▸ HOVER TIPS** turns those boxes off, and back on).
   **HELP ▸ GUIDE** opens this guide.

## The page

**The side menu** on the left picks what sits under the groovebox and the mixer:

- **TRACKS**: the song, a row a track (and LEAD), with mute, solo and the row tools (BEAT, RANDOM, VARY, CLEAR).
- **VAPOR**: slowed, warbly, dusty, crunchy, on the whole song.
- **SAMPLES**: your own sounds.
- **POLISH**: finish the mix: a compressor on one track, an EQ and an exciter on the whole song.
- **JAM PADS** is for playing over the song: the screen goes straight to the **FX keys** (stutter, tunnel, jumps,
  crush on the keys) and the two **FX PADs** come out. Back on any other panel the keys play notes again.
- **PLAYLIST** and **SHARE** open their pop-ups. Under them: what's playing, the **THEME** (the page's colours:
  the original SPELUNKR, MALL NEON, GAME CART, PUBLIC ACCESS, SNOW DAY or LATE LATE SHOW) and **POWER USER**.

The page remembers the panel and the theme. On a phone the side menu sits along the top.

**The mixer** beside the screen: a fader a track and one for LEAD (drag it up or down; kept with the song),
the small slider above it pans the track left or right (double-click: back to the middle), **M** mutes, **S** solos,
a name picks the track. **OUT** at the right shows how loud the whole song comes out; its light turns red (**CLIP**)
when it's too loud: bring a fader or VOLUME down a little.

The buttons at the top are menus: **SONG** (new, random), **AUDIO** (record live, export), **SHARE** (make a code,
open one, the playlist), **FILE** (save and open a project) and **HELP** (the tour, this guide, the manual, the hover tips on or off). **UNDO**
is a plain button.

- **SONG ▸ NEW SONG**, **SONG ▸ PRESETS** and **SONG ▸ 🎲 RANDOM SONG**: a blank song, a backing track, or a whole
  random song to play with. All three ask first, and **UNDO** brings your song back.
  **PRESETS** work like a Yamaha QY-70's styles: four for each genre (VAPORWAVE, CHILLWAVE, LO-FI, CITY POP, FUTURE
  FUNK, CHIPTUNE, ELECTRO), each shown with its tempo and key, your last pick remembered. A preset is drums,
  percussion, bass and chords in the six sections below (A intro, B main A, C main B, D break, E ending, F a fill into
  main B) arranged into a song, with **SYNTH 3** and **LEAD** left empty for your own tune. Loading one picks SYNTH 3
  and turns on *keys stay in key*, so whatever you play over it fits.
  **RANDOM SONG** writes a whole song, tune and all, in a genre the dice pick. Each genre has its own tempos, chords
  (the 7ths and 9ths, the borrowed minor IV of city pop), drum grooves, bass lines and sounds. **VAPORWAVE** is what
  vaporwave is: another genre's song (city pop most often, else lo-fi, chillwave or future funk) put through **VAPOR**,
  slowed, warbly and usually dusty; the VAPOR panel shows how much, and **OFF** takes it off. The song comes in six patterns:
  **A** the intro, **B** the A section (the verse), **C** the B section (the chorus), **D** a bridge (its own chords,
  lighter drums, a fill out), **E** the outro (thinning out and ending on the home chord) and **F** a build (the drums
  rising into the B section), arranged into a song from the intro to the outro: **PLAY** on the SONG screen plays it
  through; **PLAY** elsewhere loops the pattern you're on (B at first).
- **↶ UNDO** takes back the last thing you did to the music (a NEW SONG or RANDOM, a sound picked, a row's BEAT /
  RANDOM / VARY / CLEAR, a pattern copied, cleared or made by accident, LEAD's last take); click it again to put it
  back.
- **The song bar** under the top buttons: **TEMPO** (◀ ▶, hold to keep going, or **TAP** along 3 or 4 times),
  **SWING** (0 % straight, 30–50 % a lo-fi bounce) and **KEY**: the home note and the scale; tick **keys stay in key**
  and every key you play lands in the scale. RANDOM songs set the key they're in.
- **The screen** in the middle is the groovebox itself. The buttons on its left are named for what they do on the
  screen you are on right now (a yellow name means it does something special here). The four controls on its right
  change the four things shown along the top of the screen: tap **◀** or **▶**, or hold one to keep going (the mouse
  wheel over a control works too).
- **The screen is a touch screen**: click it with the mouse or touch it on a phone or tablet. Drag one of the four
  boxes along its top up or down to turn that control. Click a key hint, such as **OCT+ CLEAR** or **OCT- BACK**, to
  press that button. On a map of the keys, such as FX KEYS' glitch effects, touch a box to play its effect for as
  long as you hold it, just as holding its key does. On the tracker (STEPS), click a step to go to it, and click it again to turn a drum hit on or
  off; drag the tracker up or down to scroll it. In the MENU (POWER USER: hold HOME), click a row to pick it and click
  it again to change it.
- **SOUNDS** picks another sound for the track, **TRACK** picks the track, **SCROLL** moves through lists and menus,
  **VOLUME** is the volume.
- **The tracks** (the TV-guide grid under the screen): a row a track. Click a row to work on that track. **M** mutes
  it, **S** plays it alone. **BEAT** (the drum rows), **RANDOM**, **VARY** and **CLEAR** work on that track's steps in
  the pattern playing. The columns are your patterns, or, once the song has parts, the parts in order: click one
  to play that pattern next.
- **The keys** along the bottom, or your computer keyboard: **Z** to **M** and **Q** to **O** (the rows above them are
  the black keys). **[** and **]** move an octave. A MIDI keyboard works too: plug it in and look at **MIDI** in the song bar. It shows the keyboard's
  name once it's found, and lights when you play. By default the keyboard plays the track you've picked, on any
  channel; switch it to **by channel** if you want channel 1 for DRUMS, 2 for SYNTH 1, 3 for SYNTH 2 and 4 for SYNTH 3.
  If it says **ALLOW MIDI**, click it and say yes when the browser asks. Firefox asks you to add a small "site
  permission" add-on, which comes from Firefox itself. If you said no before, click the icon left of the address,
  clear or allow the MIDI permission, and reload the page. **NO KEYBOARD** means MIDI is allowed but nothing is plugged in:
  check the cable and that the keyboard is switched on.

  **LEARN** (next to the MIDI button) links the knobs, faders and pads on your controller to the page. Click
  LEARN: everything you can link gets a dashed outline. Click one, then move a knob or fader, or press a pad, on
  your controller, and they're linked (a small yellow tag shows which). You can link the mixer's faders, M and S,
  any ◀ ▶ control (VAPOR, POLISH, VOLUME and the CONTROLS knobs), TEMPO and SWING, the buttons beside the screen
  (a pad held holds the button), the side menu, POLISH's ON buttons and choices, and an FX PAD (two knobs: left-right
  first, then up-down). KNOB 1-4 follow the screen, like on a real groovebox: link one once and it turns whatever
  that knob means on the page you're on. Right-click a linked control while learning to forget it; **CLEAR ALL**
  forgets them all; **DONE** (or Esc) finishes. The links stay in this browser, from song to song (not in the song).
  The mixer's pan sliders can be linked too. Endless knobs (the kind that turn forever) are recognised by themselves:
  turn one a little after linking it and it moves the control from where it is.

## Make a song, start to finish

1. **Start**: **NEW SONG** (a blank page) or **RANDOM**.
2. **A beat**: click the **DRUMS** row and pick a kit with **SOUNDS**: CRATE and BEDROOM (dusty lo-fi), VAPOR 808,
   LINN and GATED (80s drum machines), NES, GAMEBOY and SNES (chiptune), and more. Click **BEAT** on the row for a
   beat, **RANDOM** for a random one, **VARY** to change it a little. Tap **STEPS** to see and change every step
   (a tracker: one line a step). Click a step on the screen to go to it, and click it again to turn the hit on or off.
   Move through it with the mouse wheel over the screen, the arrow keys, or by dragging it (a finger on a phone): up
   and down for the steps, left and right (or shift + wheel) for the columns. **ROW** and
   **COLUMN** under the screen do the same. **HOME** brings you back. Set the **TEMPO** and **SWING** in the song bar.
3. **A part**: click **SYNTH 1**, pick its sound, and tap **RECORD**. It asks whether you want a metronome (a bar of
   clicks counts you in, then the clicks keep time); then the music starts, and what you play on the keys goes into
   the loop. (Arm RECORD while the music already plays and there's no metronome.) Tap **RECORD** again to stop recording, **PLAY** to stop the music. To start over, **CLEAR**
   on its row. Set the **KEY** and tick **keys stay in key** first, and nothing you play sounds wrong. Do the same for
   a bass on **SYNTH 2**.
4. **Shape it**: the four controls change the track's sound. **SOUND** shows more of its settings.
5. **More patterns**: a pattern is a loop of every track. Tap **MIX**: patterns A to F are on the white keys C to A
   (computer keys Z to N). Tap an empty one, **D** (X), for a new, empty pattern B. To start from a copy instead, hold
   the key of the pattern to copy (**C**, Z, for A) and tap the key it should go to (**D**, X). Change it: a fill, a
   chorus, a drop. **UNDO** takes back a pattern made, copied or cleared by mistake.
6. **Arrange**: tap **MIX** again for the SONG screen. Tap **RECORD**, press **PLAY**, and click the patterns in the
   grid as the music goes (each one starts when the loop comes round). **PLAY** stops it, and what you played becomes
   the song's parts. **PLAY** on the SONG screen plays the song.
7. **Mix**: on the home screen, **HOME** (MIXER) gives each track's level, pan and reverb. **M** and **S** mute and
   solo.
8. **Vapor** it (below), then **export** it (at the end).

## Sounds to know

- **SAMPLE**: real instruments, aged on tape. LOFI PIANO, MALL EP, DREAM VIBES, SMOOTH SAX, GLASS PAD, KALIMBA,
  MUSIC BOX, VHS VIBES and more. Its controls: **SOUND** (the instrument), **CUTOFF** (brighter or darker), **WOW** (a
  warbly tape), **AGE** (hiss and grit).
- **GENESIS** and **SID**: the sound chips of old game consoles and computers.
- **ANALOG** and **MORPH**: classic and strange synths. **GLORP** and **FM**: wobbly and bell-like ones.

## LEAD: the voice over it all

After the last track there's one more: **LEAD**, a voice you play over everything else. Press **TRACK ▶** past the
last track (or click the **LEAD** row under the tracks), then play the keys.

- **Its sound**: **SOUNDS ◀ ▶** picks it. It starts on the theremin-like MORPH waves (it slides from note to note),
  then FM bells, the SAMPLE instruments (piano, EP, vibes, sax, glass) and the GENESIS chip sounds.
- **Its knobs**: the sound's own four; **KNOBS 2** (EDIT) swaps them for GLIDE (how far notes slide), VIBRATO, REVERB
  and LEVEL.
- **Record it**: with LEAD picked, tap **RECORD** and play. What you play is kept in the pattern that's playing and
  plays back every time it comes round, even when you've gone back to the other tracks. Record again to add more.
- **CLEAR** on the LEAD row takes this pattern's LEAD notes away; **UNDO** brings back the last take or clear.
- LEAD is saved with the song and goes along in exports and song codes.

## Your own samples

Press **SAMPLES** in the side menu. There are 8 slots.

1. Pick a slot, then **LOAD A FILE** (or drop one on the panel): WAV, MP3, OGG or FLAC. A longer file keeps its
   first 30 seconds, however big it is. **▶ ALL** and the numbered buttons let you hear it; **■ STOP** (or Esc, or the
   same button again) stops it.
2. **Trim**: drag the orange edges. **Chop**: it chops at the hits by itself, each chop ending where its sound dies
   away; **AUTO**, **4**, **6**, **8**, **12** or **NONE** chop it again. Each chop has its own **start** (the yellow
   line and its number) and **end** (the pink line and its **]** tag), with gaps between them if you like: click the
   wave to add a chop, drag a start or an end to move it (grab it anywhere in the band beside its line, top to
   bottom), double-click either to remove the chop. Up to 12 chops a sample.
3. Pick a track, then put the sample on it:
   - **AS A SYNTH**: the whole sample, played at the pitch of each key (**ROOT** is the key where it plays as
     recorded);
   - **AS CHOPS · BANK A**: a chop a key, C3 to B3. Each chop plays to its end mark however short the tap
     (one-shot, like an MPC's pads);
   - **BANK B**: another sample's chops on C4 to B4 of the same track (OCT+, or the keys past B3), beside BANK A:
     both banks play in the same pattern, at different times or together;
   - **AS A DRUM KIT**: chops 1 to 8 on the drum rows (kick, snare, clap, hat, open hat, tom, rim, cowbell).
4. **PADS**: on a track that plays chops, **▦ PADS** on the screen opens its chops as 12 pads (numbered like an
   MPC's: 1 bottom left, 12 top right), with **A** and **B** for the banks. Tap a pad to play it; with **RECORD** on,
   your taps go into the pattern. The pads light whenever their chop plays, from the steps or the keys too. **✕**
   puts the screen back.
5. **The bank's effects**, under the pads, on every chop of the bank on show (each bank keeps its own, saved and
   shared with the song): **PITCH** −12 to +12 semitones, and the speed goes with it, like an SP-303 (lower is slower
   and longer, higher faster and shorter); **REV** plays every chop backwards (a sample whose chops run past about
   20 seconds has no room to reverse: trim it); **CRUSH** fewer bits and a lower sample rate; **SAT** drives it into a
   warm distortion.

Your samples stay in this browser, and **FILE ▸ SAVE PROJECT** puts them in the project file. **SHARE** sends the ones your song
plays along with its code.

**The limits** (the panel says them too):

- 8 slots, 30 seconds a sample: a longer file keeps its first 30 seconds.
- Samples are kept in mono at 22 kHz: a little lo-fi, like an old sampler.
- Up to 12 chops a sample (a sample chopped before this kept more: it keeps its first 12). **AS CHOPS**: BANK A on
  C3–B3, BANK B on C4–B4; **AS A DRUM KIT** uses chops 1 to 8.
- The keys always reach a chop track's chops as they are: **keys stay in key** and **TRANSPOSE** don't move them.
- **AS A SYNTH** plays the sample once a note: it doesn't loop, so a long held note stops when the sample does.
- Names are up to 8 letters.
- Any WAV, MP3, OGG or FLAC your browser can read.
- They live in this browser: clearing its site data clears them. Keep a saved project file of anything you'd miss.

## Make it vapor

The **VAPOR** panel works on the whole song: **SLOWED** plays the record slower, down to half speed (lower and lazier;
it shows the speed: 100 % is as written, 50 % half), **WOW** warps the tape, **VINYL** adds crackle and hiss, **CRUSH**
makes it crunchy (◀ ▶ on each). **VAPORIZE** sets all four in one tap, different amounts every tap (tap again for
another take); **OFF** takes it all off. It is saved
with the song and heard in exports and shared links.

The drop-down beside the title picks the kind of VAPOR: **DIGITAL VAPOR** is the four controls above, emulating analog
audio equipment; **ANALOG VAPOR** has two settings modeled after Bourby's
[Fostex X-28](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/fostex-x28/7544) cassette 4-track, measured from his test tape: the song recorded at the deck's fastest speed with Dolby on, then
played back slower, with the tape's dark top end, its little bump in the lows, the way it squeezes when it's pushed,
the capstan's flutter and its quiet hiss. It has two settings: **SLOW** (the pitch knob at 12 o'clock, about 3
semitones down) and **SLOWER** (all the way down, about 6.6). It records the finished mix, after POLISH, as Bourby
does. **BOULBY NR** (on to start) is the deck's noise reduction: about half the hiss; off, the tape hisses louder.
**TRIM** (0 to +18 dB, +12 to start, Bourby's pick) is the X-28's trim: how hot the finished mix, POLISH and its exciter
included, goes onto the tape. Higher, the tape squeezes the peaks and the vibes and drums grow harmonics, the hiss sinks
under the music; the song stays about as loud.


**FX KEYS** (on **JAM PADS**, the button left of the screen): the keys glitch the whole mix while you hold them
(stutter, tunnel, jumps, crush: the screen shows which key does what); tap **FX KEYS OFF** and the keys play notes.

**EFFECTS** (the same button, on every other panel): the picked track's effects, a page at a time, tap it again for
the next: **SENDS** (how much goes to the reverb, delay and chorus, and distortion), the **SLICER** (a rhythmic gate),
the **INSERT** (one effect on the track: phaser, flanger, chorus, filter, crush, drive, ducking or a beat repeat, with its
dry / wet), **MASTER FX**, then the delay's, the reverb's (room, spring or hall) and the chorus's own settings. Holding
the button shows the FX keys anywhere.

The **FX PAD** bends the track you picked while you hold it: drag around, let go and it snaps back. Each pad has a
**LOCK**: with it on, the pad stays where you let go, on the track you played it on, until you turn LOCK off. A
locked pad is part of the song: it's still there next time, in a SAVE file and in a song code.

## Polish the mix

**POLISH** in the side menu finishes the song, like a mastering desk. Three parts, each with its own **ON**:

- **COMPRESSOR** squeezes one track (pick it under **TRACK**, LEAD too) so its loud and quiet parts sit closer
  together. On **DRUMS**, **PART** narrows it to just the **KICK** or just the **SNARE** (any kit, sampled or
  synthesized), leaving the rest of the kit alone; on **PERC**, to either of its two sounds. **STYLE**: **GENTLE** glues it together, **PUNCH** lets each hit through and holds the rest (drums snap),
  **SQUASH** is flat and loud, **PUMP** breathes with the beat (try it on the drums). **AMOUNT** is how hard; it keeps
  the track about as loud, so judge it by ear. **MIX** under 100% blends the untouched track back in. **SQUEEZE**
  shows how hard it's working.
- **SIDECHAIN** makes the tracks you pick under **DUCK** (PERC, the synths, LEAD) dip at every kick of the
  **DRUMS** and come back up in time with the beat: the classic pump. **FEEL**: **TIGHT** (a quick dip, just making
  room for the kick), **CLASSIC** (back over an eighth note), **PUMP** (the whole beat breathes) or **SWELL** (held
  down, then swelling back up). **AMOUNT** is how deep, and the **PUMP** meter shows the dip. It follows the kick's
  timing even when the DRUMS are turned down.
- **EQ** shapes the whole song's tone: **LOW** (bass), **MID** (the middle, at **MID AT**) and **HIGH** (treble),
  each up or down 12 dB, with the curve drawn above them. Presets: **FLAT**, **WARM**, **BRIGHT**, **SMILE** (bass and
  treble up, the middle down) and **LO-FI** (a small old radio).
- **EXCITER** adds new harmonics where it says: **AIR** (sparkle on top), **SHINE** (presence for leads and keys),
  **WARM** (tube-like body) or **BASS** (bass you can hear on phone and laptop speakers). **AMOUNT** is how much.

Picking a preset, style or mode turns that part on; **ALL OFF** turns all three off and keeps your settings. With the
EQ or the exciter on, a limiter at the very end stops boosts from clipping. POLISH is saved with the song: in this
browser, in a SAVE file and in a song code, and heard in exports and stems. **NEW SONG** and **RANDOM SONG** start with it off.

## Save, share, export

- Everything saves in this browser as you go.
- **SHARE ▸ SHARE THIS SONG** gives your song a short code, like `SPGB_7K2QX9A`, and a link. Whoever opens the link, or types the code
  into **SHARE ▸ OPEN A CODE**, hears your song in their own groovebox and can carry on from it. The samples your song plays go with
  it (only those, cut to their trim), into the same sample slots on their side. A code lasts **a month** (30 days from when you shared it), then it
  expires; **FILE ▸ SAVE PROJECT** keeps a song for good. Every SHARE makes a new code: if a friend opens yours, changes the song and
  shares it back, that's a new code, and yours still holds the song as you sent it.
- **AUDIO ▸ EXPORT WAV** renders the song (or the pattern) to a WAV, with a WAV a track if you like. Tick
  **Fade out** to fade the last few seconds smoothly, so a looped pattern doesn't stop dead.
- **AUDIO ▸ RECORD LIVE** records what you hear, live, to a WAV (the AUDIO button turns red and counts; STOP
  RECORDING in the same menu saves it).
- **FILE ▸ SAVE PROJECT** downloads everything as a project file, your samples too; **FILE ▸ OPEN PROJECT** loads one.

**SHARE ▸ PLAYLIST** plays up to 10 song codes back to back, like an album: paste the codes, put them in order with
▲ ▼, and press **PLAY ALL**. Each song plays its song through once (one without a song plays its pattern 4 times).
**NEXT** and **BACK** skip; your own song isn't touched, and pressing PLAY on the groovebox stops the playlist. The
playlist is kept in this browser, but the codes still expire after a month: an expired one is skipped.

## POWER USER

Without it, the groovebox shows what you need to make a song: the main sound pages and plain names. Tick **POWER
USER** for everything: the envelope, wobble (LFO), arpeggio, scale and effect pages, every sound setting, and the
device's own names (FX, SEQ, GLO, ...) on the buttons and the screen. The full manual of the device under the hood
is [the manual](manual.html), linked in the small print at the bottom of the page.

## Which browser

Use an up-to-date **Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Brave** on a computer for the best of it, and the only way to play it
with a MIDI keyboard on every system.

- **Built on Chrome** (Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, Arc, Samsung Internet, most Android browsers): the same as
  Chrome. In **Brave**, its fingerprinting protection adds a tiny, unhearable noise to sound the page reads back, so a
  sample you load there can carry it; its Shields can also block song codes: turn Shields off for the groovebox.
- **Safari, and every browser on an iPhone or iPad** (they all run Safari underneath): Safari 16.4 or newer should
  run it all, but **without MIDI keyboards** (Apple doesn't offer them to web pages). On an iPhone with the silent
  switch on, web pages make no sound: flip it off.
- **Browsers built for privacy** (Tor Browser, LibreWolf, Mullvad Browser, Firefox with "resist fingerprinting"):
  expect trouble. They turn off or scramble what the groovebox runs on (Tor's safer modes turn off WebAssembly, its
  engine, so it won't start) and can block song codes. That's their job; use another browser for this.
- **Browsers more than about three years old**: the groovebox may play, but SHARE and CODE need newer features.
